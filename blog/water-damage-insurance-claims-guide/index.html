What Policies Usually Cover

In general, a standard homeowner's policy pays for water damage when the event was accidental and happened suddenly. Typical examples are a burst pipe, an ice dam, a sink or bathtub that overflows by accident, a sudden HVAC failure, and a failed dishwasher, washing machine, or water heater. In those cases a policy usually pays for extraction, drying the structure, replacing damaged materials, and other restoration work, plus temporary living costs if the home is unlivable.

What Policies Usually Exclude

The usual exclusions are sewage backup unless you carry a specific endorsement, outside flooding unless you hold separate flood insurance, groundwater seeping through the foundation, slow leaks and other gradual damage, and anything traced to neglected or deferred maintenance. If you learn about the gaps ahead of time, you can close them with an endorsement or a separate policy.

Steps to File a Successful Claim

To keep your claim protected, begin by documenting the damage thoroughly, with pictures and video, while nothing has been cleaned up yet. Next, call the insurance company without delay and tell them what happened. Take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage, which insurers call mitigation. Hold on to ruined belongings and materials so the adjuster can inspect them first. Hire a restoration company that holds IICRC certification. Keep all receipts related to the loss, including temporary repairs and living expenses. When the adjuster visits, be there to walk them through all of the damage.
