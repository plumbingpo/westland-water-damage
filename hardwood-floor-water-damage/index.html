Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Westland

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Boards take on moisture from below and from the edges, swell against each other and start to cup, with the edges rising higher than the center. A floor in this condition is not necessarily ruined. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. The key is pulling moisture out of the boards and the subfloor underneath at a steady, controlled pace.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. The mats are sealed to the surface and connected to a vacuum unit that draws moisture up through the boards and seams, while dehumidifiers keep the room air dry. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Warning Signs

  • A springy or soft spot in the floor that was not there earlier
  • A musty smell rising from the floor with nothing visible on top
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • Finish turning white, cloudy or peeling in the wet area
  • Crowning, meaning the middle of each board bulges above its edges
  • Boards shrinking apart and leaving gaps following a rushed dry out

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Moisture Under the Boards

The subfloor can hold moisture long after the boards feel dry. Leave it wet and the cupping returns or mold grows up from below.

Rusting Fasteners

Lingering moisture rusts the fasteners, which then hold the boards less firmly. It can leave black staining around each fastener that goes deeper than a sanding reaches.

Growth Beneath the Floor

No air moves and no light reaches the gap between the hardwood and the subfloor. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. Once drying is complete, the middles sit higher and you have a crowned floor.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. You can towel off the top and still have a saturated underside.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

Boards swell and the edges rise. Finish may turn cloudy. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Day 3 to 7

Boards Start Lifting

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

Past one week

Beyond Drying

Expect a good chance of mold below, dark staining and boards that have buckled. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

Our Process

How We Handle Hardwood Floor Water Damage

1

Take Baseline Readings

We take readings on the boards, the subfloor and unaffected flooring nearby for comparison. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Extract Standing Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. Boards swell less when the seams stop taking in water sooner.

3

Set Drying Mats

Floor mats are taped down and connected to a vacuum system that pulls moisture up through the boards. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
