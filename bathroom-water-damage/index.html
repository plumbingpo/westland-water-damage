Bathroom Water Damage Help in Westland

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Westland homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

Slow leaks are the norm here, which means what you find has typically been there longer, and gone further, than it seems. Subfloor under a toilet can be soft all the way through while the tile above still looks fine. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet area without tearing out more than we need to, then open only the sections that cannot be dried in place.

What It Looks Like

  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

Each shower pushes a little water through the failed caulk or grout. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

Soft wall board, loose tile and subfloor that has rotted are removed. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.

What You Cannot See

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

A Failed Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Damage Downstairs

Water follows pipes and joists before it drips, so the stain downstairs may sit several feet from the actual leak. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.
