Kitchen Water Damage: What Westland Homeowners Should Know

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. For homeowners in Westland, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

Signs to Look For

  • Water stains or bubbled paint on the wall behind the sink or dishwasher
  • The bottom edges of cabinet side panels swelling or flaking apart
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Cabinet floor under the sink that is warped, stained, or soft to the touch
  • The toe kick boards have puffed up, turned dark, or come loose
  • An unexplained jump in water use, or dripping you can hear when nothing is running

Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

First we close the line to the leaking appliance or fixture, slide out the dishwasher or fridge when needed, and identify the exact failed connection so the repair can happen ahead of drying.

2

Measure the Wet Area

We check the cabinet bases, the wall at their back and the floor throughout the room with thermal imaging and moisture meters. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

Toe kicks come off and air is directed under and behind the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

There is no restoring cabinet boxes once they have swelled. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

The wall at the back of the sink and dishwasher holds drain and supply pipes. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Flooring Over Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Water gets trapped between the layers, where it cannot evaporate and will not show up on the surface.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Disposal seals and drain fittings that are loose leak only when water is going down, which makes them easy to overlook. A damp sink cabinet smells and grows mold quickly because that water has food waste in it.

Under the Base Cabinets

The gap under base cabinets is closed on every side. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.
