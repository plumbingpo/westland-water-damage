Wet Carpet Drying: What Westland Homeowners Should Know

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Westland, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. Carpet soaked by a clean supply line can usually be dried in place if we get to it quickly. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

Warning Signs

  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • A sour, musty smell that gets stronger when the room is closed up
  • Brown or yellow stains wicking up to the tips of the fibers as it dries
  • Carpet that has come off the tack strip, leaving loose areas or ripples

How We Fix It

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

We trace how far the water traveled with moisture meters and thermal imaging, including under walls and into closets. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

Even clean water goes bad the longer it sits. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Mold on the Backing

The dark, damp underside of the carpet is where mold usually begins. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can begin growing on the backing and on dust trapped deep in the fibers.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

Past one week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
