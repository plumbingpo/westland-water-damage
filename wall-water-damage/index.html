Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Westland

Drywall acts like a wick. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

Not every wet wall has to be torn out. When the water was clean and the drywall remains firm, drying it where it stands is often possible, at times by pulling the baseboard and pushing air into the cavity through small holes. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

What It Looks Like

  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Carpet edges or floorboards wet and discolored right next to the wall
  • Fuzzy or dark patches hidden in corners or in back of furniture
  • Damp, stale odor concentrated along a single wall
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, wrinkles or peels near the floor
  • A wall surface that gives, feels mushy or breaks apart under your thumb
Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Insulation Holding Water

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Do not use outlets on a wet wall until the circuit has been checked.

Water Next Door

It slips beneath the bottom plate and reaches the closet or room on the other side. We check both sides of every affected wall and the flooring along it.

Wet Sheathing Outside

Outside walls have sheathing past the insulation that can also get soaked. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Gypsum softens and the paper lets go. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Studs and plates stay wet long enough to grow mold and begin to decay. A far larger section of wall needs tearing out and rebuilding.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Find Every Wet Area

We scan each wall with a moisture meter and thermal camera and mark how high and how far the water traveled, including the opposite side and adjoining rooms.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Our call on each section, dry it or take it out, depends on water category, exposure time and the condition of the panel, and we explain it in plain terms.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.
