Dealing With Water Heater Leak Cleanup in Westland

Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Because the supply line feeds the tank, a rusted out bottom drains and refills at the same pace. Nothing stops the flow except shutting the cold water valve. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

Our Westland team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. When the heater is in the garage, items kept on the slab get wet and so does the wall adjoining the house. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Your First Steps

  1. Turn Off the Heat SourceIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorLift boxes, rugs and anything absorbent off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

How We Fix It

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check the platform, wall bases, adjoining rooms and any ceiling below to find the full extent of the wet area.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • Loose plumbing connections left after a recent installation or repair.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Flex connectors or inlet and outlet fittings at the top of the heater that have corroded.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

Adjacent Room Flooring

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Items Stored Near the Heater

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Attic and Ceilings

A heater in the attic sends water through insulation onto the ceiling below. Wet insulation stops insulating and its weight can bring ceiling drywall down.
